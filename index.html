Orbit determination and electrospray emitters

Graduate Student Researcher · University of Michigan

I worked on an object-oriented MATLAB orbit determination framework spanning initial orbit determination, batch weighted least-squares precise orbit determination and high-fidelity propagation, applied to low Earth orbit debris.

Initially working with optical data, post-fit angular residuals now sit below 10 arcseconds, down from more than 300 — a roughly thirty-fold improvement. My main contribution to the estimator is on the drag side: a Bayesian a priori ballistic coefficient estimate paired with an adaptive drag observability analysis, which cut drag estimation error by more than 95 percent. Knowing when drag is observable turns out to matter as much as how it is modeled.

Pivoting the modelling to radar data (no angles) allowed me to explore novel methods of orbit determination. I developed an initial orbit determination method that recovers a state estimate from range and range-rate alone using nonlinear system optimisation and hands that estimate to the precise orbit determination stage.

In the same group, I am currently working on manufacturing solid tungsten electrospray thruster emitters by electrochemical etching while simultaneously investigating porous emitter fabrication techniques aimed at long-duration emitter performance.

Foils for space-flown mass spectrometers

Research Assistant · University of Michigan

I compared carbon and graphene foils for time-of-flight measurements in ion mass spectrometers, simulating ion beams through each material in SRIM to characterize angular scattering and energy loss. Foil choice sets the instrument's mass resolution, so the trade is worth doing carefully.

I built a Python automation pipeline to run the simulation sweeps and process the output, and evaluated a new method for aligning microchannel plates in a Chevron configuration using a laser source and light meter.

Ionospheric plasma drag in low Earth orbit

Undergraduate Researcher · Singapore

I investigated how a satellite's orbital and design parameters govern charged particle drag in low Earth orbit, running more than a hundred simulations in ProPIC, a hybrid particle-in-cell code, to visualize and quantify the plasma drag around a spacecraft.

Reconfiguring Ansys EMA3D Charge across satellite geometries and orbital altitudes brought simulation runtime down from 24 hours to 5, which is what made the parameter sweep feasible at all. I contributed the formal analysis and data curation to the resulting paper in Acta Astronautica.
